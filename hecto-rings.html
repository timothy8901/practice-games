How it works

100faces
32per edge
102,400tiles
1,664rings
  • Rings circle the corners. Each of the 52 corners wears 32 rings: ring 1 is the few tiles touching the corner, ring 32 runs along the far edges of the faces that meet there. A turn slides a ring one face round its corner.
  • Turn a ring by swiping along it on the dial, with the ↺ ↻ buttons, or by dragging a tile on the 3D puzzle. Every tile rides three rings, one round each corner of its face, and the direction you drag picks which.
  • Pick a corner with ‹ ›, a corner badge on the dial's rim, or a tap on the puzzle near that corner. Cap turns every ring from the corner out to the one you touch.
  • Look around by dragging the space around the puzzle (or right-drag, or two fingers). Scroll or pinch to zoom in on the tiles; double-tap to reset.
  • Keys: [ ] corner, ↑ ↓ ring, ← → turn, C cap, Ctrl+Z undo.

The solid is the convex hull of 52 points spread as evenly as a sphere allows: the lowest-energy arrangement of 52 like charges from the Thomson problem. That gives 100 near-equal triangles, with 5 meeting at 12 corners and 6 at the other 40.
