# LANTERNFALL — Systems-First Concept

*Lens: systems. One machine, two clocks, one currency. Every number below is a starting value exposed as a data constant; the tuning table in section 7.6 is the single source.*

---

## 1. Title

**LANTERNFALL** — *A Light on the Brindle Coast*

**Tagline:** "Pay what's owed. Read what's coming. Keep the light."

**Pitch.** You arrive on foot at dusk across a tidal causeway to a harbor town where only seven porches burn and a lighthouse has been dark for thirty years. A beaver from a builders' co-op hands you a lantern, a lease card and an interest-free loan; a creature stranded on the causeway stones comes to the lantern's light and its Heartmark lights at your name. From the Keeper's Cottage at the lighthouse's foot you live two lives on one clock: by sun you pay down seven loans that grow the cottage overnight and climb the lighthouse scaffold; by tide you cross to six outer isles where creatures Shift into new forms on odds you can read, Spire leaders certify you as a Shiftreader, and a comet called the Vagary is swinging back. Every morning a snail delivers one thirty-year-old letter explaining why the light went out and why the creatures started changing shape, and they turn out to be the same night. The seventh payment and the Keeper's Trial relight the lamp; the beam steadies the Shifts, calls the fleet home, and shows the Vagary the way in.

**Alternative titles:** *The Keeper's Glass* · *Hearthmark* · *Low Light, Long Tide*

## 2. The unifying fiction

**One night, two wounds.** Thirty years ago the Vagary, a comet on a long orbit, broke apart over the Brindle Coast in a winter storm. Islanders call that night the **Ebb winter** (what it did to the harbor) and the **Night of Wrong Shapes** (what it did to the creatures). It is one night.

- The comet's head kept the **Heartshard**. Smaller fragments became **skyglass**, humming at a pitch only creatures hear. One piece, the **Weirstone**, fell into the river above the old mill weir and fused the sluice wheel shut; the river stopped scouring the bar, the bar rose every season, the oil boat could no longer cross.
- The hum put growth pressure on every creature. On the storm night the harbor's draft Bilgebobs **Shifted mid-tow** and no longer knew their people. The fleet grounded on the bar; every boat was lost.
- Keeper **Isolde Petrel** had traced both faults in her tide logs: a silent river, and a glow on the harbor creatures a day before each change. She filled the lamp from her last reserve, went upriver, and could not shift the fused wheel alone. The lamp ran dry. Young harbormaster **Ansel Stalk** wrote the report that blamed her. She left; her letters went into the sack the week the office closed.
- Every creature born since carries a faint **Heartmark** that lights comet-magenta when it bonds. **Provost Agathe Merrow**, who lost the harbor, founded the **Mooring** (anchor stones, licences, the Anchor Pulse). **Teo Varga**, who saw only the Shifted swimmers survive, founded the **Scatter** (Starsalt from skyglass dust, Shift fairs, the Carnival Raft).

**What Shifting is.** Growth meeting skyglass pressure: about three times in a life a creature may change body, Up, Side or Down, from its family pool. A Shiftreader sees the glow early and, through the **Keeper's Glass** (a lens Ambrose Hearthwright ground from the old lamp), reads it as the three-card **Shift Forecast**. Accept lets it land; Anchor (a Mooring stone) refuses it; Reroll (Scatter Starsalt) redraws the menu.

**What the Relighting does mechanically.** Isolde re-ground the lens with skyglass dust, a Lantern big enough to call the coast's creatures home, and never got to light it. Relit, the beam that sweeps the town every 8 seconds reads every Heartmark on the isles the way a Lantern reads one creature:

1. **It steadies the Shifts.** Windows fall into a posted **Shift tide table** (party screen and chalkboard). Recall opens at the Workshop. Mooring clinics run on the table.
2. **It calls the fleet home.** A ferry horn answers; the Outer Isles ferry runs; neighbors arrive two a night; Tamsin's boat floats once Ansel turns the wheel; Isolde writes "I saw the light."
3. **It shows the Vagary the way in.** The settled Vagary perches on the **Shardcrown sea stack** at night, catchable, and the beam reveals the **light road** of stepping stones at night low tide, where the ninth Spire runs.

The light went out for the same reason the creatures changed: nobody was reading the water. Reading, and paying what's owed, is the player's verb.

## 3. The player

- **Lease card** (every field visible, editable later): species (water vole, river otter, red panda, opossum, ferret, mongoose), ten fur palettes each, markings, eye shape, pronouns (separate), name with confirm, **Your Signal** (six pentatonic chimes). Wren's small talk sets only your houseplant and tomorrow's weather, and says so.
- **The Lantern.** Wren waits with a lantern and a lease card. The lantern is the co-op's old **Keeper's Lantern**: "Every Keeper carries one. It's yours with the key." It is the capture device from minute one: raise it, light draws the creature in, a three-segment meter fills. No throw, no wobble, no click.
- **The first creature.** As the sea closes over the causeway, a **Sloshkin**, a **Scrapling** and a **Tossup** are stranded on the last dry stones. Wren: "One of them's been looking at you." Raise the Lantern; the chosen one comes to the light at 100%, is named, and its Heartmark lights on the name. The other two turn up later (one with Linnet, one wild on Lowmoor).
- **Who teaches Read.** **Ambrose Hearthwright**, Wren's half-blind father, the coast's last master Shiftreader (Corin Vale merged into him). He built the original Lamp Room and reads glow by touch now; he hands over the Keeper's Glass on night one and reads the dock battle's Forecast aloud. His owl **Plumb** runs one scripted Shift per optional exhibition, one per act.
- **Who teaches Capture Stance.** **Ansel Stalk**, the heron who fishes the empty north creek every evening and never lands anything. On the quay, night one: "The trick to keeping a thing alive is deciding not to finish it." A toggle in any wild battle; party damage cannot drop the target below 1 HP; no move slot. He teaches the fishing tells too.
- **Battle identity:** Read first, check the Forecast, commit. The player's creatures never Shift in battle.

## 5. The clocks and the session

**The sun.** One in-game day = 24 real minutes by default, so **one in-game hour = one real minute**. Settings: 12 / 24 / 48 minutes, plus **Real Clock** (1:1 with the system clock; builds then take a real night, and the setting says so). Dawn 06:00, dusk 18:00: night is exactly half the day.

| In-game hour | Real minute (24-min day) | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 06:00 | 0 | Mailbox: one old letter, build note, neighbor letters. Ledger Board, Tide Table, Commons board refresh. The Thatch tie off a new roof before 06:00. |
| 08:00–22:00 | 2–16 | Pell's Sundries open; Juniper buys at full price. |
| 18:00 | 12 | Dusk. Hesper's counter opens (to 02:00). Night encounter table switches on. Rest becomes available. |
| 20:00–23:00 | 14–17 | Porch talk (life-thread beats). Porch Duels from the Commons board. |
| 20:00–02:00 | 14–20 | Porchlight Hours. |
| 22:00–08:00 | 16–26 | Ludo buys anything at full price. |
| 02:00 | 20 | Hesper closes. |
| Overnight | — | One house build, one Commons build, Raft draft refresh, Consignment Crate sale, Ambrose's carving. |

**The tide.** Two lows per in-game day, 12 h 25 min apart, each 50 in-game minutes later than the day before (50 real seconds of drift). Causeways walkable ±2.5 h of low (5 real minutes, twice a day); flats and glints ±1.5 h; stepping stones ±2 h. High tide brings shoals to the quay and Brine encounters to the sea zones. A **spring tide** every 7 days uncovers the Old Quay (Barnaby's bell) and, after the Relighting, the light road. Guarantees: a low in any 12.5-hour window; nothing exclusive to one tide; the ferry is always there.

**Night is half the game.** A night encounter table (Hollow, Glass, Rime; levels +2; Squallcoil as lightning on the sea), Prismoths at lit porches and string lights, night sea fishing as the best income (John Dory 2,400◉, lantern wolffish 4,200◉), glowing flats giving up **skyglass relics**, Ludo buying, porch talk, the Lantern Market every 7th night.

**Rest.** From 18:00 at the bed or porch (Waystones offer it from 22:00): heals the party, runs the overnight builds, writes Dimity's Day Page ending in "Tomorrow:", and skips to 06:00 in a 3-second clock spin. Before 18:00 the bed only saves. In Real Clock mode Rest never skips.

**An ideal day (Day 9, scripted in 11.2):** letter and boards at dawn → shake and dig the morning low → cross a causeway → route, Reads, catches, a Forecast at the Waystone → Spire or trainers through the afternoon → home, sell, pay before 18:00 → porch talk → night low tide: glowing flats, John Dory, a night encounter → Ludo → Rest.

## 6. Core loops

| Loop | Length | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Minute** | 1–3 s per verb | Shake, pick up, dig, fish, talk/linger, Read, fight-or-decline, Lantern. Each verb has its own sound and a torn-paper slip. |
| **Day** | 24 min | Letter → boards → tide plan → one isle leg or one town day → sell → pay → porch talk → night tide → Rest. One build and one letter per night, always. |
| **Week** | 7 days, ~2.8 h | Spring tide; Lantern Market (night 7, 14); Porch Supper (nights 3, 10, 17); Porch Sale (mornings 4, 11, 18); Archive Bounty refresh; a badge or two; a loan tier or two; a life thread finishing. |
| **Arc** | 4–8 h, 10–20 days | Act I (days 1–3): arrival, loan 1, Spires 1–2. Act II (days 4–8): Spires 3–4, loans 2–4, the Raft fair, Linnet's fall, the first Errant. Act III (days 8–12): the Surge, Spires 5–6, loans 5–6, Merrow at the Engine, Vagary. Act IV (days 12–15): loan 7, the Keeper's Trial, the Relighting. Post-game open. |

**How cozy and combat coexist.** Neighbors are never fought; on the mainland only what you fish up or walk into at night fights you, and those encounters never block a path. Combat lives on the isles and the lighthouse stair. Both halves feed one wallet and one clock: payouts pay loans, loans build rooms that train creatures, letters explain creatures, creatures speed up town verbs.

**The failure state (blackout).** When your last creature faints outside the Summit, the screen dims to porchlight amber and you wake on your porch (or the last Waystone, your choice on the card) one in-game hour later. Cost: **10% of buttons on hand, capped at 1,500◉, nothing if you hold under 500◉.** Loan progress, items, Starsalt and badges are untouched. Dimity writes "Rough night"; Wren's rule is quoted once: "You can't lose what you haven't got." Summit losses, Porch Duels, exhibitions and the dock fight cost nothing. No deadline, no scolding, no guilt.

## 7. Economy and progression

### 7.1 One currency

**Buttons (◉)**, brass, uncapped wallet, purses go straight to the wallet. **Starsalt** is the single steering currency: it buys Rerolls and Recall and nothing else, and is never sold back. Anchor stones are items priced in buttons. Wildshift's "glint" survives only as the name of the daily buried purse.

## 13. Art and audio direction (one page)

**Canvas.** 480×270 native, 16×16 tiles, integer-scaled, nearest-neighbour, UI text on a 960×540 overlay. Flat 3/4 top-down with the Skyband (sun arc, moon, tide indicator, clouds matching Augusta's odds, meteors) across the top 40 px; the camera eases down at the shore to a true horizon with the dark lighthouse on it. The lighthouse is the compass.

**Palette.** One 64-colour master: Porchlight's seasonal and night groups (darkest `#141B33`, lightest `#F6F1E3`, porchlight amber `#F9C46B`, lighthouse amber `#FFB547`, bioluminescent cyan `#3FF2E0`, ledger paper `#F4F1E6`, brass `#D9A441`, wax-seal red `#A8323A`) plus Wildshift's one reserved key, **comet magenta `m`**, only on Heartmarks, skyglass, Shift effects, the Forecast panel and the Vagary. At night the world drops to ink and deep blue; the only saturated warms are the two ambers and real flames, so the harbor is the progress bar. Time of day by palette LUT (seven states), ordered-dither crossfade, no multiply overlays; Heartmarks, skyglass, HUD and cursor never remap.

**Sprites.** People 24×32 field-guide proportions, selective outlines in each sprite's own dark hue, 12 fps over a 60 fps world, one anticipation frame per action, squash ≤ 10%. Creatures are palette-indexed string art at 32×32 (field, party) and 48×48 (battle; Vagary 64×64), left-front 3/4, silhouette test, one spottable feature, per-individual Heartmark anchor (head / chest / back / tail) carried through every form: 3 px plus at 32, 5 px plus with glints at 48, `w` highlight on cool bodies, `y` on warm or white.

**Readability.** Grass shows ground between tufts and rustles; what blocks has a base, a dark line and a shadow; ledges have a lip and chevrons; weather never covers walkable tiles (3:1 contrast floor, overlays cap at 35%, "Clear visuals" toggle, Path view). Every tell is shape + word + icon: Ward lattice and `Ward ?`, EVA and Mends pips, `EMPTY`, `Strong / Neutral / Weak / No effect / Blocked by Ward`, fish tells as a glint every 2 s or a ribbon wiggle.

**Signature motions.** Shift scramble (90 f, 4×4 blocks resolving outward from the Heartmark; reduced motion: dither crossfade), Forecast card reveal (skippable after first viewing), Lantern raise → draw-in → 3-segment meter (≈ 1.9 s, no click), send-out unfold, Wren's count, THWAP (5 f + 2 f shake + dust), Hesper's letter, Juniper's glint, Barnaby's fog, Odile's viewfinder, let-it-go.

**UI language.** Keeper's desk: ledger paper, twenty-notch tally-stick bars, wax-seal buttons, torn-paper slips, 5×7 field-guide type with dyslexia-friendly and large options. No leaf motifs. The Forecast alone carries magenta.

**Audio (procedural WebAudio).** Eight day-part themes (dawn, morning, noon, golden, blue hour, night, small hours, pre-dawn) as stems: harbor band base, night low brass, low-tide mud ticks and waders or high-tide surf and rigging, weather, battle (wild / trainer / Spire / faction / Errant / Trial), a Shift stem, glass harmonica over the glowing mud; a two-bar bridge on the hour. The 4-note Heartmark motif opens the title and every Shift sting (Anchor plays only the chord); the falling Vagary motif in Errant and faction scenes. Your Signal is the door chime, Dimity's hum, and what the ferry horn answers with. Bonded cries share a 2-note tail. Tell sounds: Ward ping, blocked tink, empty thud, save chime, save-failed buzz, Lantern hum and chord, 1 s bee hum, mid-range plip, stacking tick rising with the count, speaker ticks per trade instead of babble. THWAP is the loudest sound in the game. Sliders for music, SFX, ambience; mute key; captions for every cue that matters.

## 14. Risks, mitigations and the cut order

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Two games glued together** | One wallet, one clock, one board, one cast table; every Porchlight verb has a creature rule and every Wildshift facility a town address. Playtest question "where does the town end?" must get no answer. |
| **24-minute day makes the tide invisible or tyrannical** | 5-minute causeway windows twice a day; the ferry removes any wait; Tide Table on the door and the Skyband. |
| **Forecast fatigue or famine** | ≈ 18 Forecasts in a 6-hour run (one per 20 minutes); presets, deferral, skip after first viewing; Restless for more. |
| **Economy drifts** | Ladder and payouts are constants; the debug hook logs ◉ per day against the 7.2 targets; Porch Duels are the pressure valve. |
| **Blackout feels punitive** | Capped, floored, porch wake, loan untouched; Dimity's line is a joke. |
| **Curve vs run length** | 8L² curve, bench 60%, catch-up ×3, wild levels under curve; median-gap warning points to the board. |
| **42 forms of string art** | Family rigs shared; Star-touched are palette variants; 48×48 battle sprites; 4 frames per view. |
| **Empty night** | Six night-only things (encounters, Prismoth at lamps, glowing flats, night fish, porch talk, Ludo); night income is the best income. |

## 15. Top decisions

1. **One in-game hour = one real minute.** Every Porchlight hour becomes a number a player can plan around; the other speeds scale from it.
2. **The Ebb winter and the Night of Wrong Shapes are the same night.** One cause for the dark lamp and the Shifting, so letters, loans and badges tell one story.
3. **The Summit is the lighthouse stair; the Relighting is the ending.** Both climaxes in one building on one day.
4. **The Relighting steadies Shifts, calls the fleet, shows the Vagary in.** A mechanical payoff, not a cutscene.
5. **Buttons are the one currency; Starsalt the one steering currency.** Each has exactly one job.
6. **Ladder 8k → 80k, total 280,000◉, notch = 5%.** Sized to 14 days of rising income; a finished thread is literally one notch.
7. **Loan 1 is paid inside the 12-minute first night.** A forced-dusk prologue lands every required beat before the first letter at minute ~22.
8. **Wren gives the Lantern; Ambrose (absorbing Corin) gives the Glass; Ansel teaches Capture Stance.** Three teachers already in town, no new cast.
9. **The starter is chosen with the Lantern on the causeway.** Capture, tide and Heartmark taught in ninety seconds.
10. **Six isles, six Spires, windows at Lv12 / 24 / 38.** Wildshift's three-window shape in a 50-level run.
11. **Badges unlock tools, never passage.** The Summit gate is the only badge-locked door.
12. **Letters gate nothing but their own threads.** A grinder can relight with twenty read; the backlog keeps coming.
13. **The tide gives shortcuts only; the ferry runs at any tide.** It matters without trapping.
14. **Blackout = porch wake, 10% capped at 1,500◉, nothing under 500◉.** A failure state the cozy tone can carry.
15. **Rematch board = Commons board, three friendly Porch Duels a day.** On-demand income in the town's own furniture, damped only there.
16. **Training Yard is a room commission.** The bench facility gets a loan choice and a town face.
17. **Glowing flats give skyglass; skyglass is Starsalt.** Night low tide is the player's Shift-steering mine.
18. **Prismoths come to your porchlight.** The Ward tell is met at home before it is fought.
19. **Partner type modifies town verbs** (Brine fishing, Gust shaking, Grit digging, Glass flats, Bolt sense). One rule each.
20. **Freefall odds 30/30/40 as a placeholder constant.** Wildshift's one [NEED SOURCE] gets a number so the dial ships; the tuning table owns it.
