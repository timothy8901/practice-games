# Judge 1 — player experience and cohesion

Lens: does it feel like one AAA game; is the first hour great; does the 24-minute day have a rhythm. I read the brief and all four concepts in full and spot-checked disputed claims against the source docs (noted inline as "source:").

Source facts that settle several disputes:
- The weir was built by **Ansel Stalk's** grandfather (porchlight-story l.180); Ansel is the heron who wrote the report and fishes the empty creek. The story concept moved both to Idris via its merge; production and systems have it right.
- Wildshift's own home village is already called **Pellwick** on Lowmoor (wildshift-story l.107), so "Pellwick" as the hub name is a merge, not an invention.
- Porchlight's ladder is 12k/90k/180k/300k/450k/600k/750k = **2,382,000◉** (porchlight-rules l.289, 359). Production's 238,200 is exactly one tenth; story's 346,000 is not "one eighth" (that would be ~298k).
- Porchlight's production doc did rename the bulletin board **"the Mooring Post"** and has Ansel chalk the odds under the barometer (porchlight-production l.130, 177). Story's objection that this collides with the Mooring faction is valid.
- Wildshift's eight Spires and leaders: Turn/Maud, Ward/Osric, Disc/Dov, Weather/Ines, Hollow/Ambrose Teague, Relay/Hallorans, Mirror/Seraphine, Long Road/Hollis (wildshift-story l.625-632). Summit Keeper 1 is **Odile Strand** (name clash with Porchlight's Odile) and Keeper 3 Mirren Hale runs doubles.
- Dial odds: Drift 50/40/10, Flux 40/35/25, Freefall [NEED SOURCE] (wildshift-rules l.262, 289). Shift windows ~Lv9/20/34 on the curve levels of isles 2/4/6 (l.149, 1023).
- Wildshift names no species for Corin, Linnet, Maud, Idris or Merrow (Corin has an unnamed **owl partner**, he is not an owl). Every concept's species for them is an invention; none is "wrong".
- Porchlight flags: "Tuck", "Clem", "Bramble" collide with competitor villager names; the **river-otter** lease option and "Pell" need a naming check (porchlight-cast l.72, 223, 412).
- Challenge modes as defined by Wildshift: No Rewind (1 autosave, no manual), One Life (fainted creature leaves for good), One Heart (**only the starter may battle**), Ride the Dice (no Anchor), Blind Read (scouting off), Restless (Anchor costs 2 stones) (wildshift-rules l.931-937).
- Player sprite in the Porchlight art bible is **24×32, 2.5 heads tall** (porchlight-visuals l.114).
- Title collisions (web, 2026-10): "Lanternfall" already names at least two released small games (an itch.io horror platformer and a Halloween tower defense). "Lampfall" has no exact match. "Saltlight" is only a studio name.

### Ranking

**1. production — 68.** It wins because it is the only concept that would actually ship at AAA polish with a team of agents: 24 counted maps, a 32-key palette with 7 hour-LUTs applied at atlas build, a sprite linter, a 15-sequence tracker, 48 named sfx, a module tree with no DOM in rules or data, an economy sim that must land the Relighting on day 11-16, a battle sim with a turn ceiling, and a Playwright full run as the definition of done. Its first 30 minutes land every brief beat (creature at minute 4, Linnet's hold-out and a Forecast seen at 6:30, first catch at 17, THWAP at 23, the player's own Forecast at 25:30, letter 1 at 28). The "low-tide short way / any-tide long way" rule for every isle is the cleanest answer to "the tide never traps". Where it is weakest is exactly my lens: the Relighting's "steadied dice" is a five-point odds nudge the player cannot feel, the Champion is pushed past the credits, the ending has no script beyond four flags, loan 1 at 1,200◉ makes the first THWAP a toy, night is "half the game in content, not in minutes" (20:00-06:00), and it keeps the "Mooring Post" name that collides with the faction. It is the right skeleton; it is not the right heart.

**2. feel — 65 (tie broken on my lens).** The best opening and the best day in the set. Three mounds at the slip and the one you kneel beside picks you (agency without a menu); Corin at minute 6; Linnet at minute 7 with the first Forecast seen on someone else's creature; Isolde's lantern from Juniper's back room at minute 10 so **the capture Lantern is the lamp you will carry up the stair**; a seeded low tide at 19:30 so the first flats glow on night one; THWAP at 18:30, porchlight at 20:00, and Hesper delivering letter 1 to the newly lit porch at 21:00 so the first session does not need a Rest to get its letter. The ideal-day table in real minutes from 06:00, eight day-parts, the clock running at half rate in battle and pausing in menus, and Lie down / Nap till dusk / Sleep till dawn are the rhythm the brief asked for. Its juice list (frame counts for the Shift flip, the Lantern unfold, the THWAP) is what makes "AAA not jam" concrete. Against it: the title collides with released games, it keeps the flagged river otter and the three flagged neighbor names, four of six isles are ferry berths (the tide gates only two legs), the two-gate ending (zero balance **and** Summit flame) walls the cozy player behind six badges, and a 636,000◉ ladder inflates every number.

**3. story — 65.** The deepest and most original fiction: the Mooring kept the lamp dark **on purpose** ("light calls the change"), which finally gives the thirty-year darkness a reason the town accepted and makes relighting an argument the Provost comes round to; ten letters written in full in a voice that could ship as-is; the Reading (the Vagary as "a Forecast with a body" you cannot lose, ended only by Accept); the Lantern letter; the coda; the ninth Spire built from your own seed. It also does the hardest IP work (pine marten for the otter, a rename pass, trademark note, and its title is the only one that is clear). It loses on first-hour feel: the first creature does not appear until minute 14:30 of a 24-minute session, so the opening is thirteen minutes of pure Porchlight, and the first catch waits until minute 22. Its Idris-absorbs-Ansel merge is striking but contradicts the source (the weir is Ansel's family's) and loads the courteous Champion with the report-writer's guilt; Augusta-absorbs-Maud deletes the ferry, the brief's own "never traps" safety valve. Numbers are present but scattered, and it proposes seven Spires and four Keepers it then cuts.

**4. systems — 62.** The best tuning table (7.6 is the single page an engineer would pin up) and some of the best cohesion moves: the Summit **is** the lighthouse stair, a finished life thread is literally one tally notch, Prismoths come to your porchlight so the Ward tell is met at home, and the Relighting does three mechanical things. But it changes the fewest player-facing things for the better: Rest is offered from 18:00 and skips to 06:00, which lets a player skip the entire night the brief says is half the game; the starter is caught in the first ninety seconds on the causeway before the player has met a single neighbor; Ambrose absorbing Corin overloads the beaver who gnaws tally sticks with being the coast's last master Shiftreader; it keeps the Summit Purse (forced savings that read as a deadline); the title collides; and it leaves the flagged names and the otter in place.

## 2. DECISION LEDGER

Format: **topic** — winner (or blend) — the concrete value — why. `[judge]` marks my own answer where all four are weak.

1. **Title** — **story**, with feel's sub-title: **LAMPFALL — *A Keeper's Year on the Brindle Isles***; tagline (systems/feel) "Read the odds. Pay what's owed. Keep the light." Two lenses converged on Lanternfall, but it already names released games; Lampfall is one word for the lamp going out and the stars coming down, and it is clear.

2. **Unifying fiction** — **story's shape with production's single cause and the source's Ansel**: thirty years ago the Vagary (a comet on a thirty-year orbit) broke over the isles on the storm night of the Ebb winter; its head dug the Starfall Lagoon and threw up Shardcrown; its shards fell as **skyglass** into the river, the flats and the lagoon. Skyglass hums at a pitch only creatures hear; growth under that hum is a Shift, and every creature born since carries a faint **Heartmark**. The bar had been rising for years because **Ansel Stalk's grandfather's mill weir** stopped the river scouring it; the oil boat could not cross; Isolde Petrel filled the lamp from her last can and took her Siltback upriver to open the seized sluice; the comet came down while she was at the wheel, the Siltback Shifted under her hands, the wheel needed two, the lamp ran dry at midnight, every draft creature in the harbor Shifted mid-tow (the Night of Wrong Shapes) and the fleet grounded on the dark bar. Young harbormaster Ansel wrote the report that named her; her letters went into the sack the week the office closed. **Why it stayed dark:** no oil, no keeper, and Provost Merrow's Mooring rule **"light calls the change"** (skyglass creatures drink light), which the town accepted because nobody was paying attention. Ebb winter = Starfall = Night of Wrong Shapes: one night.

3. **What the Relighting does mechanically** — **blend (story + production + systems)**, four flags and one set piece: (1) **Steadies the Shifts**: from the next tide, Shift windows resolve only at low tide and the next low's three cards for every pending creature are chalked on the Tide Table a tide ahead (story's Lit Forecast); once per night the beam holds one Forecast opened at home for free (story/feel's Keeper's Anchor). Rejected: production's "+5 points Down→Side" (unfelt) and story's Keeper's Pip (invisible).

## 3. Contradictions and risks the synthesizer must resolve

1. **Where the Champion sits.** Feel and systems want Idris before the ending (feel: he lights your Lantern win or lose; systems: the Summit is the lighthouse stair); production and the brief's own wording ("opens the Summit") put the Summit after the Relighting. I ruled post-game (one gate on the ending, the Reading as the creature climax). The synthesizer must then make the post-game visibly *worth it* (the Summit seal on the Seed Card, Idris's anchor-release scene, the Vagary catch) or players will stop at the credits. If the panel overrules and keeps a two-gate ending, adopt feel's rule that the Champion lights the Lantern win or lose, and surface both halves on the Ledger Board ("Lamp Room: built. Flame: Shardcrown.").
