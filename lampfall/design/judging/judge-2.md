### Ranking and reasoning

**1. systems.md (63).** The only concept whose numbers close as a machine: one real minute = one in-game hour, an hour-by-hour day table, a tide with explicit windows (causeway ±2.5 h = 5 real minutes twice a day) and a ferry that runs at any tide, a ladder (8k/15k/25k/36k/50k/66k/80k = 280,000◉) checked against per-day income targets (4k → 45k/day, ≈340k cumulative by day 14, a quarter to sinks), a notch = 5% so a finished thread is literally one notch, and section 7.6, a single tuning table every engineer can type into `data/economy.js`. Its crossover tables (one rule per partner type; every Wildshift facility gets a town address) are the clearest answer to "two games glued". It loses points for three integrity faults: (a) its letter gates (bell at letter 20, Vagary foreshadow at 30, confession at 40) sit on days 20/30/40 of a 14-day arc, so none fire before the ending; (b) the Relighting requires a *won* Keeper's Trial against a Lv50 Champion, which is a battle wall on the cozy ending; (c) the first-30-minutes script needs the starter at Lv11 by minute 30, which its own EXP formula (8L², KO = yield × level / 7) cannot deliver from ~6 fights; the Lv12/24/38 windows also drift above the source's ~9/20/34. Merging Corin into Ambrose and moving the Summit into the lighthouse stair are bold and cohesive but cost fidelity.

**2. production.md (62).** The best-engineered document: 24 maps counted, 15 music sequences, 48 sfx, a module tree with no DOM below `engine/`, seeded RNG streams named per subsystem, a save schema with checksum, and an acceptance gate ("the economy sim lands the Relighting on day 11–16") that is exactly how the ladder should be tuned. Clock paused in battle and menus, buyers at every minute as a unit test, every isle with a low-tide short way and an any-tide long way: these are the right structural calls. It loses on arc: the Summit and the Vagary are post-credits, so Wildshift's climax becomes optional; a 1,200◉ loan 1 makes the first THWAP a twelve-click shrug; battle items are priced at a fifth of the source (Lantern 60◉ against fruit at 120◉), and 20 Keeper letters at one per morning do not finish inside a 14-day arc with no front-loading stated. Blackout capped at 500◉ is so small it stops being a rule.

**3. feel.md (59).** The best first thirty minutes in the pack (town verb and creature verb alternate every two minutes; creature at 5:00, Lantern at 11:00, catch by 15:30, THWAP at 18:30, letter at 21:00, pip at 22:30) and the best screen/juice/accessibility spec. Two ideas should survive into the synthesis: the capture Lantern is Isolde's lantern, and the Champion lights it win or lose. But the numbers do not close: a 636,000◉ ladder against an income table that tops out near 40–45k on a good late day (its own "80k on day 18" is not reachable from fruit 120 and fish 2,400 without grind), loan 2 at 30,000 right after a 6,000 loan 1, "54 letters, two a day, in 18 days" (that is 36 letters), the clock running at half rate in battle (a five-fight Spire eats four in-game hours and a tide window), and 72 techniques plus three Keepers on top of six Spires.

**4. story.md (56).** The strongest fiction and the strongest scenes (the dock, the Engine, the Summit's edge, the Lantern letter), the best letter plan (Keeper letters front-loaded, 18 of the first 24; the Lantern letter arrives at the Relighting whatever the count), and the best ending shape (the Vagary's "Reading" is a Forecast with a body that cannot be lost; the Summit stays the skill test). As a machine it is the weakest: no per-day income model, 7 Spires + Long Road + Summit + Engine + 7-phase Vagary + 58 letters, no ferry before badge 2 so an unlucky player waits up to nine real minutes for a low to leave town, the first creature arriving at minute 14:30 after eleven creature-free minutes, and three heavy cast merges (Idris absorbs Ansel, Augusta absorbs Maud) that each remove a source role (the ferry, the Champion's distance). Its source-faithful prices (Lantern 250/700/1,400, Starsalt 1,500, stones 1,000, cap 3,000) are right in ratio and should be kept in spirit.

## 3. Contradictions and risks the synthesizer must resolve

1. **Who lights the ending.** Systems requires a won Keeper's Trial for the Relighting; story and production need only loan 7; feel needs loan 7 plus a flame Idris gives win or lose. Resolve as ledger entry 16: money alone relights; the Summit is independent and branches the scene. Do not let a Lv50 fight wall the cozy ending, and do not let the Relighting be a cutscene with no Wildshift payoff (the Reading and the Lit Causeway are that payoff).

2. **Where the Summit is.** Lighthouse stair (systems) vs Shardcrown (the other three and the source). Pick Shardcrown and keep the Bell there; the stair belongs to the Relighting alone.

3. **Letter pacing vs run length.** Systems' letter-count gates (20/30/40) and Porchlight's ~70-letter confession cannot fire in a 14-day arc at one letter a day. Either two deliveries a day (ledger 20) or scene-based triggers (K8 read + trust). Decide and write the delivery order as a data table; add a unit test "K8 arrives on or before day 11".

4. **First-session EXP arithmetic.** With cumulative EXP = 8L² and KO EXP = yield × level / 7, a Lv5 starter reaches Lv7 after ~5 KOs and Lv11 after ~18. The first-30 script must promise the pip at Lv7 (window Lv8), not Lv11/12. Keep the window table and the EXP formula in one data module and test "starter reaches window-1 within the scripted first-session fight list".

5. **Ladder vs income.** Four ladders span 238k–636k. The tuning that matters is the ratio to per-day income (~20–30k mid-game at Porchlight prices). Adopt 280k and gate it with production's economy sim (Relighting day 11–16, no grind over 30 minutes). Watch loan 1: at 8,000 with 3,000 of errands, ~5,000 must be earned in ~15 real minutes on night one — the first-30 script's shakes, purse, honeycomb, Glint (560), trainer (300) and sales land ≈ 4,000–5,000; if the sim shows a shortfall, drop loan 1 to 6,000 before touching anything else.

6. **Battle clock.** Feel runs the clock at half rate in battle; the others pause. Pause everywhere (a Spire of five fights would otherwise consume four in-game hours and a tide window). Real Clock mode is the exception and the setting says so.

7. **Night is half the game vs skip-to-dawn Rest.** All four let Rest jump to 06:00, which Porchlight explicitly forbade ("no sleep until tomorrow"). The brief's accelerated day justifies it, but it makes night optional. Mitigate: Rest offered from 20:00 only; the Day Page lists what the night still holds (next low, Ludo, Prismoth at your lamp, a porch beat) before confirming; night income is the best income so the pull is real. Production's "porch sit ×6" is a good middle setting.

8. **Night length.** Production's night is 20:00–06:00 (ten hours); systems' is 18:00–06:00 (twelve). Use 18:00–06:00 so "half the game" is literally true by the minute, with LUT states dusk/blue hour inside it.

9. **Tide trapping.** Story has no ferry before badge 2 and ±1.5 h windows; with two lows a day that is up to ~9 real minutes of waiting to leave town. The any-tide long way plus Maud's ferry at any tide (200◉ until badge 2) must exist from day 1, and a unit test should assert every Waystone and Spire is reachable by an any-tide route.

10. **Price-scale consistency.** Production prices battle items at a fifth of the source (Lantern 60◉ against fruit 120◉); story keeps source prices (Lantern 1,400◉) against a 346k ladder. Keep one scale: Porchlight-scale town prices, Wildshift items at roughly a third of the source (ledger 12), and check every "always below one route trainer payout" rule (Crackdust) against the trainer formula per isle in a test.

11. **Summit Purse.** Systems and production keep it (2,000 or 1,500 × badges 4–6); feel cuts it as a felt deadline; story re-aims it as a loan payment. The Spire share (10% of the open loan per badge) does the "badges pay the loan" job without forced savings; cut the Purse and keep Hollis as gatekeeper.
