Rank is not harvest. The merged game should be **story.md's fiction and cast on production.md's chassis, tuned with systems.md's numbers, opened and juiced by feel.md**. The ledger below shows story.md winning the most rows.

**1. production.md (SALTLIGHT).** The only concept a team of coding agents could start building tomorrow: 24 named maps as string grids, a 32-key palette with the LUT applied at atlas build, a sprite linter, a 6-voice tracker, seeded RNG streams named per subsystem, a versioned save schema, an economy sim, a battle sim, a Playwright full run as the definition of done, a perf budget and a build order that is playable after every step. Its "every isle has a low-tide short way and an any-tide long way" rule honours the tide guarantee with no special cases. Its weaknesses are on my seat: the fiction is the thinnest (the comet and its passenger are both "Vagary" and never reconciled; the Summit and Vagary are shoved past the credits; Ansel and Idris coexist with nothing linking them), it keeps the Summit Purse (a forced-savings mechanic that reads as a deadline), and it gives the player no starter choice at all (a scripted Sloshkin at 100%), which fails the brief's "chooses the first creature" and Wildshift's Beat 1 (src: wildshift-story.md:703). Loan 1 at 1,200◉ makes the first THWAP cheap.

**2. story.md (LAMPFALL).** The best fiction by a distance: the Mooring kept the lamp dark on purpose ("light calls the change"), which gives the thirty-year darkness a reason the town accepted and turns the Relighting into an argument the Provost comes round to. Idris Fenwake absorbing Ansel Stalk is the single best merge on the panel: the man who wrote the report is the Champion who has kept his partner anchored since it hatched; the weir is his grandfather's; the confession at the Summit's edge closes both arcs in one scene. Isolde's Loupe = the Reading Glass unifies Porchlight's starter tool and Wildshift's scouting tool. It is the only concept with the letter serial actually ordered (first 24 deliveries) and ten letters written, the only one that noticed "every boat was lost" contradicts a ferry, the only one that caught the Mooring Post / Mooring faction collision, and the only one that swapped the IP-flagged river otter (src: porchlight-production.md:532, 991). It loses on buildability: 7 Spires, 4 Keepers, the Bar, Shardcrown and a Lantern Stack as three extra locations, and a first creature that arrives at minute 14:30 after thirteen creature-less minutes. Its economy is the least tabulated.

**3. systems.md (LANTERNFALL-S).** The tuning table in 7.6 is the best single artifact across the four documents: every constant with a value, the 8L² curve, catch-up, damping, bench rates, capture bonuses. Its "no single gate blocks all the rest" table (7.5) is the clearest statement of how loans, badges, letters, tide and levels pace each other, and its crossovers are one rule per type. It scores lower on this seat because its merges strain: Ambrose absorbing Corin loads the THWAP elder with the master-reader role and gives him an owl named Plumb beside a disc named Plumbline; the Summit inside the player's own lighthouse stair needs four Keepers to "ferry in" to a town with no boats; Augusta is written as an "owl warden" when Porchlight makes her a grey seal (src: porchlight-production.md:449); 15 neighbors and 11 hand-written threads are twice the brief; the Lv12 first window makes the "pip before minute 30" promise shaky under its own 8L² curve (Lv5→Lv11 is 768 EXP, about fifteen Lv6 KOs).

**4. feel.md (LANTERNFALL-F).** The best first thirty minutes (a town verb and a creature verb alternating every two minutes, everything landed by minute 24), the best ideal-day table (real minutes from 6 am), the best screens, controls, juice list with frame counts and accessibility page, and the only Day 40 script. It ranks last on this seat for three reasons: the capture Lantern *is* Isolde's lantern from minute 11, which spends Porchlight's finale beat (Juniper hands over the lantern at dusk, "Don't drop it") before the player has met Juniper properly; the ending is double-gated on loan 7 *and* the Summit flame, so a cozy player must clear a full gauntlet to relight; and the 636,000◉ ladder is two to three times the others and would make every mid-game day a 35,000◉ grind. It keeps Tobin and Wick, Maud and Augusta as separate people (the biggest cast), uses "Mooring Post" for the board while the Mooring is a faction, delivers two letters a day against Porchlight's stated rule (src: porchlight-rules.md:614), and makes no rename pass.

## 2. Decision ledger

Format: **topic** — winner (or blend) — the concrete value — why. `[judge]` marks my own answer where all four are weak.

### Title and fiction

- **Title** — systems + feel (2 of 4 chose it independently) — **LANTERNFALL**, sub-title *A Keeper's Season on the Brindle Isles* (sub-title from feel/production). Tagline (systems/feel): "Read the odds. Pay what's owed. Keep the light." One word that is the Lantern (creature half), the lamp (town half) and the Starfall (the night). Not Tidemark, not Tally & Tide. Runner-up SALTLIGHT; keep as the name of the Relighting music cue.
- **What happened thirty years ago** — story, with systems' two-wounds framing — **One night: the Starfall = the Ebb winter = the Night of Wrong Shapes.** Idris Fenwake's grandfather's mill weir had been holding back enough river that the bar rose each season; Isolde Petrel traced it in her tide logs and in a second log for "a wanderer on a thirty-year road"; the sea hummed; on the storm night she filled the lamp from her last can and took her Siltback upriver to the seized wheel; the Vagary broke over the isles while she was there; its head dug the Starfall Lagoon and threw up Shardcrown; its shards fell as skyglass into river, flats and lagoon; her Siltback Shifted under her hands and the wheel needed two; the lamp ran dry at midnight; every draft creature in the harbor Shifted mid-tow; the fleet grounded on the unlit bar; everyone waded home, every boat was lost; young harbormaster Idris wrote the report that named the keeper; her letters went into the sack the week the office closed. (Matches both sources: wildshift-story.md:133-150, porchlight-story.md:180-183; only the weir's builder changes with the Idris/Ansel merge below.)
- **What the Vagary is** — story (resolves production's double meaning) — **The Vagary is the comet; "the Vagary" is also what islanders call the creature it carries**, and the game treats them as one visitor: the comet climbs the Skyband a little each night through Acts II–III; on the Relighting night the passenger descends to the lit stack. The Field Log plate reads *Vagary (the Passenger)*, Star type, ability Unfixed, never on a Forecast card.
- **What skyglass is** — all four agree — shards of the comet that hum at a pitch only creatures hear; in the water now, so every creature born since carries a faint four-point Heartmark that lights comet-magenta on bonding; the Scatter refines its dust into Starsalt; the glowing night flats give it up as relics.
- **Why the light stayed dark** — **story** — three reasons stacked: no oil could cross the bar and no boats remained; the keeper was blamed and gone; and **Provost Merrow's Mooring kept it dark on principle ("light calls the change": skyglass creatures drink light), and the town accepted it because nobody was paying attention.** Paying attention, and paying what's owed, is the player's verb. This is the one answer that makes relighting an argument rather than a chore.
- **What the Relighting does mechanically** — blend: story's rules 1–4, production's rule 5, feel's beam — paying loan 7 triggers dusk on the point; Juniper hands over Isolde's lantern; you and Wick carry the flame up; the lens catches; the beam sweeps every roof every 8 s. Then five one-line state flips: (1) **Lit coast**: the Shift Tide Table — every pending window is chalked on the Tide Table by your gate a tide ahead and on the Day Page's new *Shift Tide* line, and five points move from Down to Side on every dial (Drift 50/40/10 → 50/45/5; Flux 40/35/25 → 40/40/20), after Bond; the dice still roll. (2) **Keeper's Anchor**: once per night the beam holds one Shift free on a Forecast opened at home. (3) **The fleet comes home**: the ferry horn answers with Your Signal; the dark houses reopen one per night; Tamsin's boat floats; the Outer Isles ferry runs post-game with Linnet at the wheel. (4) **Light calls the change**: the Vagary descends to the lit stack for the finale and perches there after, catchable; Errants come to the lit point at night (1 in 120); Star-touched forms roll at 4% under the beam. (5) **The lit Bar**: the drowned tide-road from the point to Shardcrown is walkable at any tide. Each is a flag in the save.
