# LANTERNFALL — concept document (lens: player-feel-first)

Feel seat. Built from the WILDSHIFT and PORCHLIGHT reference docs and the orchestrator brief. Numbers are starting values; screens are drawn for the 480x270 native canvas.

---

## 1. Title, tagline, pitch

**LANTERNFALL** — sub-title *A Keeper's Year on the Brindle Isles*.

Tagline: **"Read the odds. Pay what's owed. Keep the light."**

Pitch: You walk into Pellwick at dusk, the last person to cross the causeway on foot, and a beaver with a lantern hands you a lease card and the key to a cottage under a lighthouse that has been dark for thirty years. That night a mud-flat creature picks you, its Heartmark lights comet-magenta when you name it, and a half-blind Shiftreader presses a brass lens into your paw. From then on you live two braided lives on one clock: by day you shake trees, fish the quay, dig the flats and walk the tide-gated causeways to six isles whose Spires test how well you read creatures that change shape on visible odds; by night the harbor becomes Porchlight Hours, where someone is always buying, neighbors talk on their steps, the mud glows cyan and a snail brings the evening post from a thirty-year backlog. Seven interest-free loans grow the cottage overnight and climb the lighthouse scaffold; six badges and a Summit earn the flame; the seventh payment carries your own capture Lantern up the stair and relights the coast. Nothing is hidden, nothing is punished, nothing is timed, and every session ends on a list that begins *Tomorrow:*.

Alternative titles: **The Keeper's Lantern**; **Wickmoor**; **Heartmark Harbor**.

## 2. The unifying fiction

**One night, two wounds.** Thirty years ago the comet islanders call the Vagary broke over the Brindle Isles. Its heart struck the sea and made Starfall Lagoon; its dust fell as skyglass. The same night was the storm of the Ebb winter. Keeper Isolde Petrel had traced the harbor's trouble upriver, a mill weir holding back the river so the sandbar rose and the oil boat could not cross; she filled the lamp from her last reserve and went up the valley to open the seized sluice. While she was gone the skyglass came down. Every draft creature in the harbor Shifted mid-haul (the Mooring calls it the Night of Wrong Shapes), the lamp ran dry, and the fleet grounded on the bar in the dark. Everyone waded home; nobody sailed again. Young harbormaster Ansel Stalk wrote the report that blamed the keeper. She left; her letters explaining the weir went into the post sack the week the office closed; the lamp stayed dark because the town that needed it was gone.

**Shifting.** Every creature born since the Starfall carries a faint Heartmark. When growth meets skyglass pressure it Shifts, Up, Side or Down, permanently, from its family pool; the mark lights magenta only when bonded and keeps its spot through every form. A Shiftreader sees the glow lean; the Reading Glass turns the lean into three cards with odds. Anchor stones (Mooring) hold a Shift; Starsalt (Scatter) shakes the cards. Nothing is worn, nothing is timed.

**The Vagary returns.** Through the middle acts the comet climbs the Skyband a little each night, the only magenta in the sky. Wild Shifts quicken on the outer isles (the Surge). Merrow's Sinking Engine and Varga's charges race it to the Rimeholt sea stack; you read the Vagary's own seven Forecasts there and end it by Accepting. Shifts start following a tide table. That is Act III, not the ending.

**What the Relighting does.** The Lamp Room (loan 7) is built overnight but its lens needs two things: a zero balance and a Lantern that burns with Summit fire. The Brindle lights were lit in a chain that begins at the brazier on Shardcrown, kept alone for thirty years by Champion Idris Fenwake; when you reach him at the end of the gauntlet he lights your Lantern from it, win or lose (winning also unhooks his anchor ring). At dusk on the day the balance hits zero you carry that same Lantern, the one that has drawn every creature you own into light, up the stair with Wick. Then, in rules: (1) **the beam steadies the shapes**: a dithered amber cone sweeps every roof every 8 s; wild Surge Shifts stop inside its reach; each night it grants one free Anchor on a Forecast opened at home; the Day Page gains a Shift Tide line naming tomorrow's window. (2) **It calls the fleet home**: a ferry horn answers with your Signal; the remaining dark houses reopen one per night; Tamsin's boat; Isolde's post-credits letter, *"I saw the light. —I. Petrel."* (3) **It opens the post-game**: the Vagary perches and can be caught; Idris walks the ring with whatever Vesperay has become; the Seed Card prints.

**Why no pacer gates the rest.** Loans 1 to 6 are money-only. Badges open the Summit. Letters gate only Barnaby's bell and Ansel's confession. Shifts gate nothing. Only the ending needs two things, and the Ledger Board says which half is missing in Wren's hand: *"Lamp Room: built. Flame: Shardcrown."*

## 3. The player

**Lease card.** Wren Hearthwright meets you at the causeway's end. Every field visible: species (water vole, river otter, red panda, opossum, ferret, mongoose), ten fur palettes, markings, three eye shapes, blush, outfit, pronouns as a separate field, your name, the town's name, and **Your Signal** (six notes on six pentatonic chimes). Wren's small talk sets your houseplant and tomorrow's weather, and she says so. Controller-first name entry with confirm; full preview before signing. The Chaos dial sits on the card's back ("Weather of the isles: Drift / Flux / Freefall / Custom", Flux default, Drift marked *gentler*) with the seed code and challenge seals.

**First creature (minute 5).** At the slip three mounds breathe in the warm mud: a Sloshkin (Brine), a Mudfist (Grit), a Rushling (Moss/Brine). Kneel at one and it pops up to the eyes and follows you; kneel at all three if you like; the one you keep kneeling beside is the one that picked you. Corin Vale, a tawny owl with failing eyes who came in on Maud's ferry to test readers, waits on your step. On the name the Heartmark lights magenta (first magenta pixel of the game, the 30 f glint, the four-note motif). He gives you the **Reading Glass** and teaches **Read**: "Look before you swing. Then say what you see." The other two stay at the slip and can be caught later.

**The Lantern (minute 11).** Corin says a reader needs a lantern; Juniper Pell fetches the only one left on the point from her back room: the keeper's. The capture Lantern *is* Isolde's lantern. Grades are wicks at Pell's (tallow 200, whale-oil 600, skyglass 1,200, each +5% odds).

**Capture Stance (minute 14).** At the first wild Tossup in the river-mouth reeds Corin walks you through the raise, the odds line ("62%, and it only goes up if you miss") and the Stance toggle: your damage cannot drop a wild creature below 1 HP, no slot, no timing.

## 5. The clocks and the session

**One real second is one game minute**: a 24-minute day (settings 12 / 24 / 48; Real Clock mode uses the machine's time). The clock runs on the overworld and in battle (at half rate, so a fight costs about 40 game-minutes) and pauses in every menu, dialogue, shop, Forecast and Day Page, because reading is never taxed.

**Two tides** 12 h 25 m apart (12.4 real minutes), drifting 50 game-minutes a day, so every day has a new shape. Low opens the flats (glints, cockles, the one Glint), the stones, the cairn causeway, and the Old Quay on spring tides; high crowds the quay with shoals. Any 12.5-hour window holds a low; nothing is exclusive to a tide.

**Day-parts**, each with a theme: dawn 5–7, morning 7–11, noon 11–14, afternoon 14–17, golden hour 17–19, blue hour 19–21, night 21–2, small hours 2–5. Blue hour plus night is half the clock.

**Sleep.** The bed offers *Lie down* (heal, 10 game-minutes), *Nap till dusk*, *Sleep till dawn*; the porch bench offers *Sit* (Day Page, save, no skip). Builds happen while you sleep; stay up and the Thatch tie off your roof at 5:40 in front of you.

## 6. Core loops

**Minute:** shake, cast, dig, talk, linger; on a route: read, fight or decline, catch. Every verb 1–3 s with an anticipation frame, its own sound and a torn-paper slip.
**Day:** letter → harvest and sell → one leg or Spire → payment → Porchlight Hours → evening letter → night tide → Day Page. Every day leaves one open thread the player chose.
**Week (7 game days, about 3 hours):** Monday beats, Wednesday parcel, Saturday Lantern Market and Porch Supper, Sunday Porch Sale, the Archive Bounty, a spring tide every 14 days.
**Arc (about 18 game days, 6–8 hours):** seven loans, six badges, 54 letters, about 18 Forecasts, the Relighting, the post-game.

**Cozy and combat coexist by geography and by cost.** The town is safe: no wild encounters, no trainers, neighbors never fought. Isles carry every fight. The only town battles are **Porch Duels**, friendly one-creature matches at porch talk from the eight neighbors who keep a creature (Tuck's gull-chasing Scrapling, Juniper's Wirewhisk, Odile's Prismoth, Linnet's Sootling); they cost nothing to lose, pay 50◉ a level, give bench EXP and move threads.

**What each pays into the ladder.** Badges pay a purse (3,000◉ x badge number) and unlock tools; trainer payouts scale with the curve; EXP never becomes money, it becomes Spires; resident shares (5% of the current loan) are friendship's payment. The cozy player and the battler reach loan 7 by different roads at a similar pace.

**Failure.** A blackout fades 40 f; you wake on your porch at the next hour with Dimity's slip, *"You came home on the ferry. Maud didn't charge."* Cost: 10% of buttons capped at 1,500◉ and the daylight. Summit losses cost nothing; the Bell remembers you once.

## 8. Crossovers

**Porchlight systems that become creature-aware** (bonuses, never locks): a Brine lead reveals a fish's tell on the first catch instead of the third, and Shoresense names the shadow; a Gust creature out of its Lantern shakes from two tiles and drops two fruit; a Bolt creature hums near purse trees and the Glint; a Stone creature marks today's cracks on the map; a Hollow creature marks glowing relics on the map at any tide (dug only on the night low); a Moss creature takes a Greenhouse graft in one night; moths and Glass-type wilds gather under string lights you hang, and a Prismoth visits your lit porch once a night; eight neighbors keep a creature and talk about yours by nickname; three of Isolde's letters describe the Night of Wrong Shapes; four flats relics are skyglass shards that double as tilts; Augusta's forecast shows isle weather after badge 4; the Day Page lists Shift pips under *Tomorrow:*.

**Wildshift systems that become town-aware:** Shelter = your cottage (isles get Waystone Shelters with beds); Rematch board = the Mooring Post request slate (Porch Duels now, leader rematches post-game); Training Yard = the Side Room; Memory relearning = the Workshop bench; Still Knots woven on the Cellar loom from clinic-sold Stillox hair; shop = Pell's (wicks, stones, Starsalt, Ethers, Crackdust, discs with learner strips); the Carnival Raft = the Saturday Lantern Market; Keepsakes = Juniper's back room; dossiers pinned on the Mooring Post the week you can reach that Spire; skiff = Maud's ferry with a destination card and *No* first when heading back; journal stamps share the letters' journal; the Seed Card prints on the tally wall.

## 13. Art and audio direction (one page)

**Look.** Field-guide pixel at 480x270, 16x16 tiles, integer-scaled, crisp. Characters 2.5 heads tall on a 24x32 rig; creatures hand-encoded as palette-indexed string art at 32x32 (24x24 icons) with four anchor regions so the Heartmark keeps the individual's spot through every form. One 64-colour master palette, hue-shifted ramps, no pure black (night ink `#141B33`) or white (`#F6F1E3`). Comet magenta only on Heartmarks, skyglass, Shift effects, the Forecast panel and the Vagary; porchlight amber `#F9C46B` and lighthouse amber `#FFB547` the only saturated warms at night, so the night harbor is the progress bar. Time and weather by palette LUT (seven states), never multiply; Heartmarks, skyglass, HUD and cursor never remap. Grass shows ground between tufts and rustles; anything that blocks has a base and a shadow; overlays cap at 35% with a Clear Visuals toggle. UI is a keeper's desk: ledger paper, tally wood, brass, wax seals, torn slips, a 5x7 face with distinct I/l/1. Characters at 12 fps over a 60 fps world, one anticipation frame per action, squash under 10%, no rotation; trees sway in four frames and the shake answers the sway.

**Sound.** All WebAudio: two pulse leads, a triangle bass, noise percussion, one FM voice, light reverb. Eight day-part themes as stems (season, weather, tide, night, battle) with a two-bar bridge on the hour; low brass after dark, glass harmonica over the mud, surf and rigging at high tide, mud ticks and waders at low. The four-note Heartmark motif lives in the title, the home theme and every Shift sting (charge, granular scramble, chord; Anchor plays only the chord); the Vagary's falling phrase haunts Errants and factions. Bonded cries share a two-note tail. Speakers tick in their trade (Wren woodblock, Ambrose bowed string, Juniper glass bead, Hesper paper, Barnaby brass, Corin a soft hoot). The Lantern hums, chords, never clicks. Sliders for music, sfx, ambience, UI; mute key; captions.

## 15. Top decisions

1. **The capture Lantern is Isolde's lantern and it relights the coast.** One light carries both climaxes; the ending has been in the player's hand since minute 11.
2. **The Relighting is the ending and needs loan 7 plus the Summit flame.** Both arcs matter and the Ledger names the missing half.
3. **The Champion lights your Lantern win or lose.** The money arc is never walled by a battle; winning still ends Idris's story.
4. **The Ebb winter and the Night of Wrong Shapes are one night.** One cause for the dark lamp and the Shifting.
5. **Buttons only; Starsalt steers.** One currency with one steering currency, as the brief allows.
6. **Ladder rescaled to 636,000◉ over about 18 game days.** Porchlight's ladder was a 40-hour calendar; this is a 7-hour arc with a 7-night floor.
7. **Loan 1 is 6,000 and paid by minute 19.** The THWAP and the porchlight must land in session one.
8. **Two letters a day, 6 am and the lamp-lighting.** Keeps the morning ritual, gives the night its own, fits 54 letters.
9. **Six Spires, three Keepers, one Champion.** Fills a ring; the Relay survives as a doubles Keeper.
10. **The town is safe; the isles carry every fight.** Porch Duels are the only town battles and cost nothing.
11. **Blackout = home on the ferry, 10% capped at 1,500.** A failure that returns you to the porch.
12. **The clock runs in battle at half rate and pauses in menus.** Time matters on a route; reading is free.
13. **Three mud creatures; the one you kneel by picks you.** Agency without a menu.
14. **Linnet is your next-door neighbor and her rival arc is a life thread.** Porch talk gives the Down Shift a place to hurt and heal.
15. **Rematch board = Mooring Post; Shelter = cottage; Training Yard = Side Room; raft = Lantern Market.** Every facility already had a building.
16. **Creature chores are bonuses, never locks.** A Gust creature shakes farther; nobody needs one to harvest.
17. **The Summit Purse is cut.** Forced savings read as a deadline.
18. **The beam's power is one free Anchor a night and a Shift Tide line.** The light steadies the shapes in a rule you can feel without touching the dial.
19. **Every session ends on *Tomorrow:*.** Dimity's list is the real save prompt.
20. **Default Flux, first window at Lv8.** The first Forecast should carry a real Down card and be close enough to promise on night one.
