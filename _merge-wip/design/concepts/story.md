# LAMPFALL — concept document (story-and-world-first lens)

Prepared for the merge panel. Source material: the Wildshift and Porchlight reference docs (rules, creatures, story, cast, visuals, production). Everything below is a proposal; numbers are starting values for the systems lens to tune. Where I overrule a source, I say so and why.

---

## 1. Title, tagline, pitch

**LAMPFALL** — *Pay what's owed. Read what's coming. Light what's dark.*

You walk into a harbor town at dusk, the sea closing over the causeway behind you, and take an interest-free loan on the Keeper's Cottage at the foot of a lighthouse that has been dark for thirty years: the night the comet called the Vagary broke over the Brindle Isles and its skyglass fell into the river and the sea. Since that night every creature born on the coast carries a Heartmark and Shifts as it grows, and the harbor's working beasts, Shifting in the dark with nobody to read them, lost the fleet on the bar. You are the new Keeper and, it turns out, a Shiftreader: someone who can see a Heartmark's glow and call the odds before a Shift lands. By day and by night you shake trees, fish the tides, dig the glowing flats, befriend neighbors with their own lives, and walk the tide-gated causeways to six isles, their Spires and the Summit on Shardcrown, catching and steering creatures with a Lantern. Seven loans grow your cottage and climb the scaffolding; a thirty-year-old letter arrives every morning; and the seventh payment relights the lamp on the night the comet comes back, so that this time the coast can read what is coming.

Alternative titles: **Skyglass Harbor**; **The Ebb Light**; **Wick & Waystone**.

## 2. The unifying fiction

**One night, two wounds.** Thirty years ago (the "Ebb winter") two slow problems met one fast one. Slow problem one: Idris Fenwake's grandfather had built a mill weir on the north creek; the mill died but the weir held back just enough river that the estuary stopped scouring the bar, and the bar rose every season until the oil boat could not cross at half tide. Keeper Isolde Petrel worked that out with a tide log and a pencil; the first letter says so ("The light is fine. It's the river that's wrong"). Slow problem two: the Vagary, a comet on a thirty-year orbit, was coming in low, and the sea had started to hum at a pitch only creatures heard. The fast problem: a storm. Isolde filled the lamp from her last reserve, took her Siltback upriver to turn the seized sluice wheel, and the comet broke over the isles while she was there. Its head struck the deep beyond the bar and threw up **Shardcrown**; its shards, **skyglass**, fell into the river, the flats and the lagoon. Isolde's Siltback Shifted at the wheel and no longer knew her; the wheel never turned; the lamp ran dry at midnight; every draft creature in the harbor Shifted in one night (the **Night of Wrong Shapes**); the fleet, towed home by beasts that had forgotten their people, grounded on the unlit bar. Everyone waded home. Every boat was lost. Idris, the young harbormaster, wrote the report that named the keeper. Isolde's letters explaining it went into the post sack the week the office closed. She left for the Outer Isles, where she became the first person to read the glow on purpose, and taught Corin Vale.

**Why the light stayed dark.** No oil came (no boats, no bar lit); the keeper was gone; and Agathe Merrow, the harbor nurse who lost her patients' bonds and her neighbors' boats in one night, founded **the Mooring** on one rule: *light calls the change*. Skyglass creatures drink light (Prismoths do; so does the Vagary), so the Mooring kept the lamp dark and licensed anchor stones cut from Cairnhold granite. The town accepted it because nobody was paying attention. Paying attention, and paying what's owed, is the player's verb.

**What Shifting is.** Growth meeting skyglass pressure. Skyglass is in the water now, so every creature born since carries a faint Heartmark that lights magenta when it bonds; a Shift is permanent, driven by growth (about three windows per creature), forecast before it lands (three cards, Up / Side / Down, odds shown; Accept, Reroll with Starsalt, Anchor with a stone), and it never touches level, EXP, held item, nickname, Bond, trained stats or the Heartmark technique. Shifts resolve on the spot or at the next **Waystone**, which here is the tide post at each causeway end. Starsalt is skyglass dust refined by **the Scatter** (Teo Varga), dug from the glowing night flats; anchor stones are quarried on Cairnhold and licensed by the Mooring. Every Forecast choice is a small vote nobody scores.

**What the Relighting does.** The lamp lit on the night of the comet's return turns the lighthouse into the coast's Reading Glass. Mechanically, from the Relighting on: (1) **the Lit Forecast** — Shift windows resolve only at low tide, and the next low tide's three cards for every pending creature are chalked on the Tide Table by your gate a tide ahead, so the whole town can plan; (2) **the Keeper's Anchor** — once per day the beam holds one Shift for free (Merrow's wish, granted gently); (3) **the Keeper's Pip** — every bonded creature gains one Bond pip above the dial cap; (4) **light calls the change** — the Vagary descends to the lit Lantern Stack for the finale and perches there afterward, catchable; Errants come to the lit point at night (1 in 120); Star-touched forms roll at 4% under the beam; (5) **the horn** — the mainland ferry answers your Signal, and the coast is on the map again (new arrivals, the Outer Isles as a post-launch door). Emotionally: the Night of Wrong Shapes happened because the coast was dark and unread; you have made it lit and readable, and the comet that caused it is met instead of feared.

**How the four pacers avoid gating each other.** Loans gate the house and the scaffolding only. Badges gate chaos tools (Reroll, Lantern-paths, Prism, Recall, tilts, Plumbline, the Summit). Letters gate nothing; they explain. Shifts gate nothing; they happen. The two tracks are stitched by money: Spire prizes feed the loan (the **Keeper's Purse**, below), and the finale needs the loan while the Summit needs the badges, so a player who neglects one finishes the other first and the dialogue branches (Porchlight already branches Idris's last line on lamp state).

## 3. The player

**Lease card.** Species: water vole, pine marten (replaces the flagged river otter), red panda (default), opossum, ferret, mongoose; ten fur palettes; markings, three eye shapes, blush, outfit; pronouns separate; name (default **Sorrel**) and town name (default **Lampfall**) controller-first with confirm; **Your Signal**: six notes on six pentatonic chimes; **the dial**: Drift / Flux (default) / Freefall / Custom, phrased by Wren as "How do you like your odds? Augusta will want to know." Wren's small talk sets only your houseplant and the first morning's weather, and she says so.

**The Lantern.** Wren meets you at the causeway holding it too high. She hands it over with the key: "Co-op issue. Finds your door. Finds other things, I'm told; ask the owl." It is the Hearth Lantern (grade 1). Grades 2 and 3 (700 / 1,400◉) are at Pell's. After the Relighting, **Isolde's lantern** becomes the one sure-catch Keepsake, confirm with No as the default.

**The first creature.** Your porchlight comes on when loan 1 is paid, on night one. At dawn three creatures are sitting under it, drawn by the first new light on the coast in thirty years: a **Sloshkin** (Brine), a **Scrapling** (Grit) and a **Reedkit** (Moss, Sedgehare's hatchling). You pick one and name it; its Heartmark lights on the name. The other two are catchable on Brackmoor by day three.

**Who teaches.** **Corin Vale**, a barn owl with failing eyes and Isolde's old apprentice, walks the causeway at the first low tide ("Nobody walks. Two of us in one week; the bar must be nearly a road"). He hands you Isolde's Loupe, which is the **Reading Glass**: it names a find on the spot and turns Heartmark glow into odds. He teaches **Read** on the dock, **Capture Stance** on the flats an hour later, and reads the odds aloud in one exhibition match per act. **Augusta Skerry** teaches Reroll at her practice table with no clock. **Wick** tags along for all of it and is the next reader.

**Tide gates.** The day has two low tides (section 5). Stepping stones to Brackmoor open at mid tide; causeways to all six isles open at low tide; the Bar to Shardcrown is dry only during the lower of the day's two lows (still every day). Every causeway has a **Waystone** at each end (autosave, deferred Shifts, a tide chalkboard, a bench that heals the party). If the sea comes up under you, you wade to the nearer Waystone at no cost ("The sea came up. You waded."). From badge 2, **Lantern-paths**: Ludo has lit every visited Waystone and you walk the lit path between any two instantly, at any tide. Commons projects (footbridges, the Old Pier) add all-tide walking routes to the two nearest isles. Nothing is exclusive to one tide; the tide gives shortcuts and bonuses.

## 5. The clocks and the session

One in-game day = 24 real minutes by default (one real second = one game minute); settings 12 / 24 / 48 and Real Clock. Menus and dialogue pause the clock. Seven day-parts, each with its own theme: dawn 5–8, morning 8–11, noon 11–3, golden 3–6, blue hour 6–9, night 9–2, deep night 2–5. **Night is half the game**: 6 pm to 6 am is 12 real minutes.

**Two tides.** Lows every 12 h 25 m of game time (12.4 real minutes), drifting ~50 game minutes later each day; low-tide window (causeways dry) ±1.5 h around each low; mid tide (stepping stones) ±3 h; high tide brings shoals to the quay and the river becomes a routing puzzle. A night low tide makes the flats glow cyan: glowing relics, skyglass dust, rare bugs and night creatures (Glass and Hollow types) spawn only then — but the same species spawn elsewhere by day, per the guarantee.

**Anchors of the day.** 6 am mailbox (one old letter, the Ledger Board, the Tide Table, the Commons board with Augusta's odds, cracks, beats, the day's six rematches, Wanted Today). 8 am–10 pm Pell's (buys at full price; Lanterns, stones, Ethers from badge 3, the Sealed Crate). 6 pm–2 am Hesper's counter. 8–11 pm porch talk (life-thread beats; neighbors bring their creatures; Porch Duels). 10 pm–8 am Ludo buys anything at full price and sells Starsalt (max 3 a visit). Saturday night Lantern Market (tilts, lamps, moth-silk). **Rest** (bed or porch) is offered from 9 pm: heals the party, rolls the clock to 5:50 am (the Thatch tie off your roof), writes the Day Page, autosaves. Before 9 pm Dimity says "Too early. Someone's always buying."

**An ideal day** (mid-game) looks like section 11.2: a morning on the isles while the tide is out, noon selling and paying, golden hour on a Spire, blue hour home for porch talk, a night low tide on the glowing flats, Ludo at one in the morning, Rest.

## 6. Core loops

**Minute.** Shake / fish / dig / talk (Linger) / Read / fight-catch-or-decline. Each action 1–3 s with its own sound and a torn-paper slip.

**Day.** Letter, boards, tide-out on an isle (route, trainers, a Waystone), sell and pay, porch talk, night flats, Rest. The day must leave one thread you chose open overnight (a build, a graft, a fossil on the bench, a deferred Forecast at a Waystone, a creature in the Yard, tomorrow's letter).

**Week** (seven in-game days, ~3 hours). Monday beats land for the neighbors you are involved with; Wednesday sister-town parcel; Saturday Lantern Market and Porch Supper; Sunday Porch Sale; weekly Archive Bounty (three wanted specimens, one of them a Held form); a Spire leg or two; a loan tier.

**Arc.** Four acts (section 9's letter table and section 7's loan/badge ladder); 4–8 hours; then the post-game.

**Cozy and combat coexist by address.** Neighbors are never fought; porch fights are **Porch Duels** (friendly, EXP yes, no payout, no cost, and a Bond beat if the creature is in your party). Trainers live on the isles. Wild encounters are in rustling grass and marked tide pools; Read is free before every trainer fight; optional trainers can be declined. **Failure state:** a blackout returns you to your porch at the next hour; you lose 10% of your buttons (cap 3,000), "dropped in the mud" — half of it surfaces again as extra Glints over the next three days. Summit losses cost nothing; the Summit Bell holds your place on Drift and Flux. No deadline on any loan, no scold, no guilt.

## 8. Crossovers

**Porchlight systems made creature-aware.** Fishing: a Brine creature in the lead reveals tells after one catch; Undertow at the quay raises the reach's busy value; a Bolt lead hums when a night headliner is near. Shaking: a Gust lead shakes two trees; Skein Sweep nets a bee nest safely. Digging: a Grit or Stone lead opens a crack in one press; a Glass lead shows glowing relics by day. The flats: glowing relics are skyglass; dust refines to Starsalt; shards polish into tilts at the Workshop. The Glint: sometimes a creature. Wanted Today and the Archive Bounty list Held forms. Porch talk: neighbors bring creatures; Porch Duels; thread beats are Bond beats. Life threads each carry a creature (Tuck's Scrapling, Tamsin's Creelwight, Bramble's Sedgehare in the beans, Odile's Shiftlog portraits, Juniper's Still Knot heirloom, Barnaby's Field Log). Letters: family letters delivered to a lit porch return an heirloom and, often, a creature ("the Dorrows' Bilgebob came home with the kettle"). The scaffolding, the Lamp Room and the beam (section 2). The Day Page lists Shift pips. Rest at your lamp is the Shelter heal.

**Wildshift systems made town-aware.** Shelter = your cottage (heal, storage in the Cellar, dial change with Dimity), Pell's (stock), Ambrose's bench (Memory relearn free, "hands remember"; Recall), the Commons board (Rematch board, dossiers posted here the day before you sail), the Yard (a commission). Waystones = tide posts. Spires = the ends of six tide roads; prizes pay the loan. Trainers = isle folk; rematches by board, never by call. Errants roam the isles and, after the Relighting, come to the light. Field Log and Shiftlogs live in the Archive; the Seed Card is Dimity's last Season Book page. Discs = the Sealed Crate. Starsalt from the flats; stones from the quarry. Bond from life-thread beats and porch talk with the creature out. Save = Dimity, verified, red "Not saved" banner. The Summit Bell hangs on Shardcrown; the fleet's bell is Barnaby's house.

## 11. Scripts

Fifty-six letters, fixed order, one each 6 am, plus two event letters. **1 (K1), To the Keeper.** "If you're reading this, someone has reopened the post, and I owe them an apology for my handwriting. The oil boat turned back again. **2 (F), To the Dorrows, cliff stair.** "Maren, your Bilgebob towed the oil boat off the bar twice this week and will not take a fish for it. I have told him he is a civic institution. **5 (K3).** "Three nights of the sea humming. Not the wind. The creatures hear it before I do; the Siltback sits on the gallery rail facing south-east like a dog at a door. **10 (K6).** "Oil for nine nights. The boat will try the bar at the full moon. **13 (K8).** "To whoever keeps the light after me. The night of the storm I filled the lamp from the last can and took the Siltback up the valley. The wanderer came down while we were at the wheel. ## 16. Top decisions

1. **Title LAMPFALL** — one word that is both the lamp going out and the stars coming down; neither sibling term.
2. **The Ebb winter is the Starfall night** — one storm, one dark lamp, one comet; both pitches' wounds share a cause, so the letters can explain both.
3. **The Mooring kept the lamp dark on purpose ("light calls the change")** — gives the thirty-year darkness a reason the town accepted, and makes relighting a quiet argument the Provost comes round to.
4. **The Relighting makes the coast readable (Lit Forecast, Keeper's Anchor, Keeper's Pip, light calls the change)** — the lighthouse becomes the Reading Glass for everyone; the ending changes play, not just the sky.
5. **The Vagary comes to the light on the Relighting night and is "a Forecast with a body" that cannot be lost** — Porchlight's no-failure ending holds, Wildshift's "theme as a boss" survives, and the Summit stays the real skill test.
6. **Idris Fenwake absorbs Ansel Stalk** — Champion, harbormaster, author of the report, anchored partner: Wildshift's unnamed fear gets Porchlight's confession, and one scene closes both arcs.
7. **Wick absorbs Tobin Marsh** — the child who calls you Keeper gets Rillfin, the water pack, the hardest fight on the Bar and the next apprenticeship; the puffling fledges across the game.
8. **Augusta Skerry absorbs Maud Tallis** — the warden of honest odds is the natural Turn Spire teacher; no ferry exists until the horn answers.
9. **Linnet Rask is a starter neighbor and a stoat** — next door from day one; her return in Act III is winter, so she comes back ermine instead of with a cut ponytail.
10. **Buttons are the one currency; Starsalt steers; the Glint is a purse** — each name keeps one job; Wildshift's glint becomes Porchlight's daily ritual.
11. **Loans 6k/15k/30k/45k/60k/80k/110k = 346,000** — Porchlight's shape at one eighth, sized to 4–8 hours at 24-minute days.
12. **The Summit Purse becomes the Keeper's Purse and pays the loan from badge 4** — the two tracks share money so they converge without gating.
13. **Spire prizes feed the ledger and Shelter functions live in town (porch, Pell's, Ambrose's bench, the Commons board, the Yard commission)** — every Wildshift building has a face you already know.
14. **Waystones are tide posts and Lantern-paths replace the skiff** — fast travel comes from the lamplighter and the tide guarantee, not boats the town does not have.
15. **The glowing flats are skyglass; anchor stones are Cairnhold granite** — Starsalt and Anchor come from the two clocks, not from shops alone.
16. **Three starters under the first lit porch** — the light draws creatures from minute fourteen, and the choice is made on your own step.
17. **The Reading Glass is Isolde's Loupe** — Porchlight's starter tool and Wildshift's scouting tool are one object handed down by the absent keeper.
18. **Twenty Keeper letters front-loaded in a 56-letter fixed order, plus the Lantern letter at the Relighting** — the mystery resolves inside a short run; the backlog still outlasts the credits.
19. **Six hand-written threads, each with a creature in it** — Porchlight's neighbors stay the heart, and every thread touches the creature loop at least once.
20. **The Commons board keeps its name; the Mooring is only the faction** — one word, one meaning, across the whole UI.
