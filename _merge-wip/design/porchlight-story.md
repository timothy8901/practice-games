## 1. The premise

You arrive on foot at dusk, which nobody does anymore, crossing the tidal causeway to the Lamplit Coast as the sea closes behind you. Here a lit porch means someone lives here and they're home, and only seven of nineteen are lit.

Wren Hearthwright of Hearthwright & Daughter Building Co-operative meets you with a lantern, a lease card and the key to the Keeper's Cottage, at the foot of a lighthouse dark for thirty years.

The morning after your own porch lights, a night-shift snail delivers the first letter of a thirty-year backlog:

> "The light is fine. It's the river that's wrong."

### Design intent (as stated)

- The story exists to serve the daily loop.
- The story comes in the mail; the neighbors have lives that move only when you're involved.
- Total written content: about 2–3 hours of scenes plus roughly 120 short letters. All of it can be skipped.
- There is no villain, no deadline and no way to fail.

## 4. What happened here

### Timeline

| When | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Two generations ago | Ansel Stalk's grandfather built a mill weir upstream. The mill is gone, but the weir still holds back just enough river that the estuary stops scouring itself, and the bar rises every season |
| The Ebb winter, thirty years ago | Keeper Isolde Petrel, a storm petrel, had traced the trouble upstream in her tide logs, and the oil boat hadn't crossed in weeks. On the night of the storm she filled the lamp from her last reserve and went upriver to open the sluice, but she couldn't shift the seized wheel alone. The lamp ran dry, and the fleet grounded on the bar. Everyone waded home, but every boat was lost. Ansel, who was the young harbormaster then, wrote a report blaming the keeper, and she left. Her letters explaining it went into the post sack the week the office closed |
| Afterward | Families drifted away, pawning what they couldn't carry, and Juniper Pell bought every heirloom before the mainland could |
| Twenty years ago | Ansel found his grandfather's weir records. He has kept quiet ever since |
| Thirty years ago to now | The lighthouse has been dark; the post office closed with Hesper's last sack undelivered |
| Now | Hesper has reopened the post office on the night shift; you arrive on foot at dusk |

### The thematic statement (verbatim)

> No one here is wicked. The town went dark because nobody was paying attention. Paying attention, and paying what's owed, is the player's verb.

### Causal chain (for designers)

1. The weir upstream holds the river back.
2. The river stops pushing the sandbar away; the bar rises every season.
3. The oil boat can no longer cross reliably; the lamp runs low.
4. On the storm night the lamp ran dry; the fleet grounded on the bar; every boat was lost.
5. Isolde was blamed by Ansel's report and left; her explanatory letters sat in the sealed post sack for thirty years.
6. Families left; heirlooms were pawned to Juniper; houses went dark.
7. The fix is to (a) deliver the backlog, (b) relight the lamp, and (c) open the weir wheel, which Ansel does himself once the Relighting and his confession have both happened.

## 5. Who you owe, and why it matters

### The co-operative

- You owe a builders' co-operative, **not** a landlord: Hearthwright & Daughter Building Co-operative.
- **Ambrose Hearthwright**, an old beaver with failing eyes, built half the houses on this coast, the original Lamp Room among them.
- **Wren**, his daughter, is precise, anxious and secretly funny. Your cottage is her first solo build.
- Since the town emptied, the co-op has had nothing to build. Your loan is their bet that the coast can come back to life.

### Terms

- No interest. No deadline.
- Wren's line: **"Pay it when you can. We build while you sleep."**
- Scene 1 quotes loan 1 as **"Twelve thousand buttons, no interest."**
- Scene 2 (paying loan 2) has Wren counting **"Ninety thousand"** on the abacus, the beads clicking once per thousand.

### The tally stick ritual

1. Each payment is notched into a tally stick.
2. The stick is split so that each side keeps a matching half (Ambrose does this with his tail: THWAP).
3. Your halves hang on your wall "like a diary of your year." The co-op's halves go "in the book."
4. Finished life threads also produce tally sticks: the resident brings Wren a stick worth 5% of your current loan.

### Design intent (as stated)

- The loan ladder is the spine of every session (O7, O15), but WW gave no reason to care who was counting. Here you watch Wren grow into her masterpiece, and your final payment lights the coast.
- The debt theme will be tone-tested with players who carry real debt [NEED SOURCE: playtest].

## 6. The central thread: Hesper's sack

### Background

- Thirty winters ago Hesper Coil was the junior clerk, and the last sack was hers to deliver, but the office closed before she finished her round.
- Now she has reopened the post office on the night shift.
- She closes at 2 am and reaches the point by 6, because she's a snail.
- Her porch (the post office) lights when you carry in the mail sack in week 1.

### Composition of the backlog

| Group | Approx. count | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Isolde's letters | ~40 | To the town and to "the Keeper" |
| Family letters | ~40 | To neighbors' families |
| Town history | ~40 (the rest of ~120) | Recipes, gossip, a furious complaint about a misprinted tide table |
| **Total** | **~120** | **Fixed order** |

### Routing: where each letter goes

| Letter type | Destination | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keeper letters | Yours (Isolde to "the Keeper") | Read in your box |
| Town letters | The Commons board | Public |
| Family letters | You carry them to the right door | Once that family's letter has been delivered to its door **and** their porch is lit, Juniper returns that family's heirloom |
| The one exception | Isolde's lantern | Juniper holds it back for the Relighting |

### The tracker

- Each relic dug from the flats links to a letter.
- Barnaby pins both relic and letter to the string wall in the Archive.
- A letter on the string wall missing its relic is a visible state (Tamsin's thread, beat 3).

### Delivery rules (the serial spec)

1. **One backlog letter on each in-game date you load.**
2. Delivered at **6 am**, or **on first load after 6 am** on that date.
3. **Skipped dates never accrue.** There is no catch-up pile for days you didn't play.
4. **A clock change can add at most one** letter.
5. **Hesper only delivers to a lit porch.** Her first delivery is therefore the first morning after your porch lights (loan 1 paid).
6. Until your porch lights, **Wren's errand clipboard writes the Day Page.**
7. Letters arrive in a **fixed order**, about 120 in total.
8. After the credits the backlog keeps coming until it is exhausted; the morning the last backlog letter arrives triggers Augusta's "Late Delivery" party notice.
9. Hesper's sack can also carry a letter that is **not** from the backlog (post-credits: Isolde's "I saw the light").

### Letter-count triggers (collected from across the section)

| Trigger | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| First morning after your porch lights | First backlog letter: "The light is fine. It's the river that's wrong." |
| Letter 30 | Says where the ship's bell went down. At the first spring-tide low after it, you dig the bell out of the Old Quay and Barnaby moves into it |
| About 60 letters read | On Tova's next visit she mentions a storm petrel on the Outer Isles who asked after this coast |
| About 70 letters read, and Ansel trusts you | Ansel confesses at the north creek (Scene 3) |
| Last backlog letter arrives | Augusta stomps up a notice: Late Delivery, tonight, on the green, with Hesper as guest of honor |
| After the credits | A non-backlog letter postmarked last week from the Outer Isles: "I saw the light. I. Petrel." |

### Sample letter (verbatim; Isolde to the Keeper)

> To the Keeper,
>
> If you're reading this, someone has reopened the post, and I owe them an apology for my handwriting. The oil boat turned back again. Its keel touched the bar at half tide, where last spring it cleared at low. The sea isn't building that bar. The river has stopped pushing it away. I'll walk up the valley Sunday and see what's gone quiet. Keep the wick trimmed.
>
> I.

## 8. Life threads

### Rules

- **13 hand-written lead threads:** seven for the cast and six for neighbors (the four starters and the first two arrivals).
- **Templates** cover the rest of the 24-neighbor pool.
- Each lead thread runs **4–6 beats over 3–6 real weeks** and **only moves while you're involved**.
- When a thread finishes, that resident brings Wren a **tally stick worth 5% of your current loan**.
- Beats happen in **porch talk (8–11 pm)**, at **Porch Suppers** and **in the mail**.
- Threads never fail; an ignored thread waits.
- Beats trigger on thread progress, never on a date. The two beats set by the tide (Tuck's Mudlark Mile, Barnaby's bell) wait at most for the next spring-tide low.

### Cast threads (detail as given)

**Wren.** From her father's book to her own Lamp Room. Milestones scattered through the section: at loan 2 she slides her own blueprint across the bench ("That one's mine. It's not from the book"); at loan 3 she shows you her own design; at loan 6 she offers the Lamp Room; taking loan 7 builds it overnight; Ambrose walks it by touch and tells her it's better than his.

**Hesper.** Shame, then fear, then a party. Shame: the undelivered sack (and reading outgoing mail she shouldn't, in Tamsin's thread; mortification when Clem lifts her letters' plot). Fear: not detailed in this section. Party: the morning the last backlog letter arrives, Augusta posts "Late Delivery, tonight, on the green," with Hesper as guest of honor.

**Ansel.** The confession and the wheel. Confession triggers once you've read about 70 letters and he trusts you (Scene 3, the north creek). Once the Relighting and the confession have both happened, Ansel turns the weir wheel himself. Order is free: the confession can land before or after the Relighting, and the scene's closing line changes accordingly (see Scene 3).

**Juniper.** Heirlooms going home. She returns a family's heirloom once that family's letter has been delivered to its door and their porch is lit. She holds back one item, Isolde's lantern, for the Relighting. Clem's bound novel appears on her shelf. Her Wanted Today board gives Tamsin an honest outlet for duplicates.

**Wick.** Starts calling you "Keeper" once loan 1 is paid. At the Relighting, you and Wick carry Isolde's lantern up the stair.

**Barnaby.** The Archive's string wall links relics to letters. House upgrade: at the first spring-tide low after letter 30, you dig the ship's bell out of the Old Quay and he moves into it (previously he lives in his helmet). He is upset when a letter on the string wall is missing its relic (Tamsin, beat 3). He can pay Tamsin a finder's fee. He hangs Odile's gallery in the Archive.

**Ludo.** Counts the lit porches. "Bats don't need lighthouses. I'd still like to see it." Roosts under the eaves of the net loft; no porch. Ferro sets up beside him at the Lantern Market.

### Neighbor threads (verbatim beats)

**Tamsin Brack, "The Oyster Boat."** She digs at twice your speed and makes sure you know it.

1. She races you to glints at low tide.
2. You see her posting a heavy parcel to a mainland dealer. Hesper tries not to read outgoing mail, fails, and worries.
3. A letter on the string wall is missing its relic, and Barnaby is upset.
4. On the glowing flats one night she explains. The Brack oyster boat, the one hull salvaged off the bar, was sold up the coast to pay the family's debts, and she is buying it back one relic at a time.
5. You help her do it honestly: a finder's fee from Barnaby, or her duplicates on Juniper's Wanted Today board. The relic comes back. The town hauls the boat over the causeway on rollers, and it rests on the mud until the harbor can float it.

(Coda: once the weir turns and part of the flats becomes shallows again, Tamsin sails the first boat in. "Tamsin's secret comes out" is listed under Chapter 2.)

**Clementine "Clem," "The Murder at [Town]."** She's always smiling, and her paws are always inky. ([Town] is the player's chosen town name.)

1. A chapter arrives in the mail, and you're in it as "the Newcomer, who walked."
2. She sends you to collect alibis from three suspects, who are neighbors in thin disguise and have opinions about it.
3. The plot is clearly lifted from Hesper's letters, and it's uncomfortably close to the truth. Hesper is mortified.
4. Clem reads it aloud at a Porch Supper. You vote on who did it, the last chapter names your choice, and a bound copy appears on Juniper's shelf.

## 10. How the story respects a cozy loop

1. **Story never gates tools, shops or upgrades.** The ledger gates exactly one scene: the Relighting, at dusk on the day your balance reaches zero.
2. **Nothing fails.** An ignored thread waits, and an unread letter stays in the box.
3. **No beat waits on a date.** Beats trigger on thread progress. The two set by the tide, Tuck's Mudlark Mile and Barnaby's bell, wait at most for the next spring-tide low, which comes twice a month. Festivals add flavour but never gate a beat.
4. **Order is free.** Ansel's confession can land before or after the Relighting. A grinder may finish the ladder having read about 20 letters, and the rest keep coming after the credits.
5. **Absence isn't punished.** Dimity's "While you were away" page catches you up.
6. **The opening says what it decides.** WW's taxi interview silently set the player's face and gender (O1). Wren tells you that her small talk sets only your houseplant and the weather.
7. **One staged scene.** The prologue is always dusk on a rising tide, whatever the real clock says. It's the one stated exception to Pillar 1 (real clock, real tide), and it hands over to the real clock and the real tide when Wren gives you the key.

### Implementation implications

| Rule | What the system must do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Only the Relighting checks the ledger balance (== 0). No tool, shop or upgrade checks story state |
| 2 | Threads have no expiry or failure state; mailbox never discards unread letters |
| 3 | Beat triggers are thread-progress flags; tide-gated beats poll for the next spring-tide low |
| 4 | Confession and Relighting are independent flags; Scene 3's last line branches on lamp state; backlog continues post-credits |
| 5 | Dimity summarises elapsed events on return; no decay or penalty |
| 6 | Lease card: every field visible and editable later; small talk writes only houseplant + tomorrow's weather |

## 12. Three scenes in the writing voice

Reproduced in full. These set the tone for all written content.

### Scene 1. The causeway (the first two minutes)

*Dusk, whatever the real hour. The Newcomer steps off the last wet stone as the rising sea covers the causeway. Seven porches glow across the harbor. A lantern bobs ahead, held too high.*

**WREN:** You walked. *(She looks past you at the water.)* Nobody walks. Ten more minutes and you'd have swum. Wren Hearthwright, of Hearthwright and Daughter. I'm the daughter.

*She holds out a card on a string. The lease card fills the screen, every field visible.*

**WREN:** You can change any of it later. I don't like hidden boxes. *(beat)* I'll make small talk while you fill it in. It decides one plant on your windowsill and whether tomorrow's sunny. Nothing else. I'd want to know.

**WREN:** Favorite hour?

> Dawn. / Dusk. / The middle of the night.

*Up on the cliff, the lighthouse stands black against the last light.*

> What's wrong with the lighthouse? / What's the catch?

**WREN:** *(lighthouse)* It's been dark thirty years. Ask Ansel. *(beat)* Actually, don't ask Ansel.

**WREN:** *(catch)* Twelve thousand buttons, no interest. Pay it when you can. We build while you sleep. *(beat)* That's the whole catch. I checked.

*She presses a brass key into your paw.*

**WREN:** It's later than it looks. Sleep.

*The sky and the sea settle to the real hour and the real tide.*

### Scene 2. The co-op yard (paying loan 2)

*Shavings everywhere. Ambrose sits on a sawhorse with his cane across his knees. Wren stands at the abacus.*

**WREN:** Ninety thousand. *(The beads click once per thousand, slowly at first, then in a blur.)* Ninety. Dad.

**AMBROSE:** Stick. *(He runs a thumb down it, reading the grain.)* Alder. Good. Notch it wide so they can read it from across a room.

*He gnaws, and chips fly. Then he lifts the stick and brings his tail down. THWAP. The screen shakes, dust puffs up, and the stick lies in two clean halves.*

**AMBROSE:** Your half. Hang it where you'll see it in the mornings. Ours goes in the book.

**WREN:** From the next tier, you choose what we build. *(She slides a blueprint across the bench, then pulls it back an inch.)* That one's mine. It's not from the book.

**AMBROSE:** *(without looking up)* I heard that.

**WREN:** You were meant to.

> That's my favorite. / Show me all of them.

**WREN:** *(favorite; her ears flatten, then lift)* You don't have to say that. *(She writes on her clipboard.)* I'm writing down that you said it.

### Scene 3. The north creek (Ansel's confession)

*Evening. Clear water over empty stones. Ansel stands on one leg, the way he has every evening since you arrived. He strikes and misses, as he always does.*

**ANSEL:** You've been reading her letters. Hesper reads slowly and talks fast. *(He doesn't turn around.)* Did she write about the wheel?

*A long pause, filled by the creek.*

**ANSEL:** My grandfather's weir held the river back year after year, and the bar rose to meet it. She worked that out with a tide log and a pencil. I worked it out twenty years late, from a drawer. *(beat)* I wrote the report that spring. It named the keeper.

**ANSEL:** Every evening I fish a creek I know is empty. I suppose I thought standing here long enough would count for something.

> It wasn't you who built it. / You should tell them. / *(Say nothing. Stand with him.)*

**ANSEL:** *(after silence)* ...That's what she'd have done.

**ANSEL:** *(lamp still dark)* I'll go up to the wheel. Not in the dark, though. She had to do it in the dark.

**ANSEL:** *(lamp already lit)* Light's on. No excuse left, then.

### Scene mechanics worth noting

| Scene | Mechanic |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Forced dusk and rising tide regardless of real clock; lease card shown with every field visible and editable later; small talk sets only houseplant and tomorrow's weather; "Favorite hour?" has three options (Dawn / Dusk / The middle of the night); two optional questions; handover to real clock and real tide after the key |
| 2 | Abacus beads click once per thousand; tally stick notched and split by Ambrose's tail (screen shake, dust); from the tier after loan 2 you choose what the co-op builds; Wren's own blueprint is distinguishable from "the book" |
| 3 | Triggered at ~70 letters read + trust; player has three responses including silence; the closing line branches on whether the lighthouse lamp is already lit (Relighting done) or still dark |

### Voice notes drawn from the scenes

- Short declaratives. Humor is dry and understated ("That's the whole catch. I checked.").
- Stage directions in italics; *(beat)* for pauses; branching responses as a single blockquote line separated by slashes.
- Characters speak around their feelings rather than naming them (Wren writes down that you said it; Ansel fishes an empty creek).
- Silence is always an option in a hard conversation, and it is the one the absent Keeper would have chosen.
- Physical comedy lands in sound and screen effects (THWAP, dust, shaking screen).
