How it works

12faces
5per edge

48rings
  • Rings circle the faces. Each of the 12 faces wears 4 rings: ring 1 is its center and the ten tiles round it, ring 2 its rim, and rings 3 and 4 the first and second rows of tiles across its five neighbors. A turn spins a ring a fifth of the way round.
  • Turn a ring by swiping along it on the dial, with the ↺ ↻ buttons, or by dragging a tile on the 3D puzzle. Most tiles ride two or three rings, and the direction you drag picks which.
  • Two dials. The ring dial draws the chosen face's rings as circles, ring 1 in the middle and ring 4 round the outside, with the edges between its neighbors marked across rings 3 and 4. The button beside the face picker swaps it for the face with its five neighbors unfolded flat around it.
  • Pick a face with ‹ ›, a neighbor's badge on the dial's rim (or, unfolded, a tap on the neighbor itself), or a tap on the puzzle. Cap turns a ring along with everything inside it: Cap on ring 3 turns a whole face, the way a real Gigaminx does.
  • Look around by dragging the space around the puzzle (or right-drag, or two fingers). Scroll or pinch to zoom; double-tap to reset.
  • Keys: [ ] face, ↑ ↓ ring, ← → turn, C cap, V dial view, Ctrl+Z undo.

The solid is the regular dodecahedron, and its faces carry the Gigaminx pattern: two cuts beside every edge, so each edge has five tiles along it (corner, wing, middle, wing, corner) and each face 31. Every cut is the trace of a plane parallel to a neighboring face, so every ring turns as a true rotation.
